# Between Birth and Death: What Makes a Life Meaningful?

We enter the world without choosing our birth, and none of us can avoid the certainty that life will one day end. Between these two points lies a space that belongs to us: the time we are given, the choices we make, the people we touch, and the awareness with which we experience it all.

The beginning and the end may be fixed. **The meaning of the journey is not.**

## Two certainties, one open question

Birth begins our story. Death brings our personal experience of the world to a close. We spend much of life trying to control countless things—other people's opinions, our status, our possessions, and the future. Yet the most important question is often simpler:

**How am I using the time I have?**

A long life is not automatically a meaningful one, just as a short life is not automatically an empty one. Meaning cannot be measured only in years, money, recognition, or achievements. It can also be found in kindness offered quietly, responsibilities fulfilled honestly, knowledge shared, and courage shown during difficult moments.

## The space between is where life happens

We sometimes treat life as if it will begin after the next milestone: after we earn more, receive approval, buy something important, or finally solve every problem. Goals can give direction, but postponing all happiness until a future date can make us overlook the life already unfolding.

Life is happening in ordinary moments: a conversation with someone who needs to be heard, work done with care, a mistake honestly acknowledged, a child learning something new, a seed planted, or a few minutes spent in quiet reflection.

These moments may appear small, but a life is largely made of them.

## Make your time reflect your values

No single purpose has to fit every human being. One person may find meaning in raising a family, another in teaching, creating, discovering, serving others, or understanding the world. The important thing is to choose consciously rather than living entirely by comparison or expectation.

A few questions can help:

- **Am I present?** Or am I moving through each day without noticing it?
- **Am I growing?** What can I learn from today's successes and mistakes?
- **Am I contributing?** Does my presence make anything a little better for another person?
- **Am I living by my values?** Do my daily actions reflect what I say matters to me?
- **Am I waiting for life to begin?** What meaningful thing can I do today, however small?

You do not need perfect answers. Asking honestly is already a way of becoming more aware.

## The Soulism perspective: awareness gives direction

Soulism places conscious awareness at the centre of a meaningful life. Its principle, **“Awareness is the Highest Authority,”** invites us to examine our choices instead of simply following habit, fear, social pressure, or unquestioned beliefs.

Awareness is not merely thinking about life; it is noticing how we are actually living. It means recognising our emotions without letting every emotion command us, examining our assumptions, accepting evidence, and taking responsibility for the effects of our actions.

It also means remembering that we are finite. Knowing that time is limited need not make life frightening. It can make our priorities clearer. We may become less willing to waste precious days on needless resentment and more willing to express gratitude, repair a relationship, learn something useful, or begin work that matters.

## Meaning is practised, not merely discovered

We often imagine that somewhere there is one grand purpose waiting to be found. Sometimes purpose emerges gradually, through experience and reflection. Sometimes it is created through the commitments we make and the care we bring to them.

You do not have to change the world to give your life meaning. You can begin by being truthful when a lie would be easier, patient when someone is struggling, curious when you could be dismissive, and brave enough to change when you discover you were wrong.

Small choices do not remove life's uncertainty. They help determine what we do with it.

## A final thought

We cannot choose every circumstance of our birth, and we cannot know exactly when our journey will end. But today, while the journey continues, we still have opportunities to learn, love, create, help, and become more conscious of the life we are living.

The question is not only how many days we will have. It is what we will bring to those days.

**Birth gives us a beginning. Death gives life a boundary. What we do in between gives our journey its character and meaning.**
